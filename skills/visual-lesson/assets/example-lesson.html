How do we find the lowest point of a curve?

Many machine-learning models learn by searching for the lowest point of an error curve.

Think of the curve as a valley and of the ball as someone looking for its lowest point. The blue line shows how steep the curve is under the ball, and this steepness is called the slope.

Because the slope is negative on this side, the curve goes down to the right, so the ball moves right.

Why look for the lowest point? A machine-learning model has settings, and for each choice of settings we can measure how wrong its answers are. This measure is called the error.

When we plot the error against one setting, we often get a curve shaped like this valley. Training the model means finding the setting at the bottom, where the error is smallest.

Real models have millions of settings, so their error has millions of directions instead of one. Each step of the search still works the same way as on this curve.

The ball does not roll smoothly. It moves in steps, and each step is the slope multiplied by a number that we choose, called the step size. The orange line shows every step from the start to the bottom.

Drag the slider and watch the path and the step count. Try a value above 1, and then one above 2.

What you should see. With small steps, the ball creeps down and needs many steps. With larger steps, it gets there faster.

Above 1, each step is so long that the ball jumps past the bottom to the other side, and then back again. It still settles, but in a zigzag.

Above 2, each jump lands higher than the one before, so the ball never settles. Choosing a step size that is too large is a common reason why training a model fails.

Writing one step as a formula

new position=old position−step size × slope
xnew=x−η·f′(x)

To take one step, we start from the old position and subtract the step size times the slope. We subtract because the ball must move against the slope, which is the downhill direction.

In symbols, the step size is written η and the slope is written f′(x). Hover over them for details.

Check: which way does the next step go?

The slope at the ball is −2.5. Which way does the next step go?

Subtracting η × (−2.5) adds a positive number to x, so the ball moves right, which is downhill.

Hint: a negative slope means that the curve goes down to the right.

Now the ball is on the other side, where the slope is +2. Which way does it go?

Subtracting a positive step makes x smaller, so the ball moves left, which is downhill.

Hint: the ball always moves against the slope.
